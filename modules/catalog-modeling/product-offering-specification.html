Section 2.1

Product Offering vs Specification

The difference between what you sell (Offering) and what it is (Specification)

One of the most fundamental concepts in catalog-driven telecom architecture is the distinction between a Product Offering and a Product Specification. While they are closely related, they serve entirely different purposes and live on different sides of the commercial-to-technical boundary.

Offerings Reference Specifications
Offerings reference Specifications. One Specification can be referenced by many Offerings.

Product Offering (PO)

A Product Offering is the commercial representation of a product — what the customer sees, selects, and purchases. It is the sellable entity in the catalog and contains all the commercial attributes that drive customer engagement, ordering, and billing.

Key Attributes of a Product Offering

  • Name & Description: The customer-facing title and marketing description (e.g. "SuperFibre 200 Home").
  • Pricing: One-time fees, recurring charges, usage rates, discounts, promotions. The offering references a pricing table or tariff structure.
  • Channel Eligibility: Which sales channels can sell this offering (online, retail, partner, direct sales).
  • Customer Eligibility: Segment restrictions (residential vs business), credit checks, geographic availability.
  • Bundling Rules: Whether the offering is a standalone product, a bundle, or a component of a bundle. Includes mandatory and optional components.
  • Validity Periods: Start and end dates for when the offering can be sold.
  • SLAs: Service level commitments associated with the offering (e.g. 24-hour repair, 99.9% uptime).

In TM Forum terminology, the Product Offering is the entity that sits in the Product Catalog and is consumed by CRM, CPQ, and Order Management systems.

Product Specification (PS)

A Product Specification is the technical definition of a product — what the product actually is, independent of how it is packaged, priced, or sold. It describes the product's characteristics, its relationship to services, and the rules for how it is composed.

Key Attributes of a Product Specification

  • Characteristics: Technical parameters such as bandwidth, data cap, latency class, technology type (FTTH, DSL, 5G).
  • CFS Linkage: The service specification(s) that must be instantiated to deliver this product. This is the critical bridge from commercial to technical.
  • Composition Rules: How the specification decomposes into sub-specifications. Defines the structural hierarchy.
  • Compatibility: Which other specifications can be combined with this one in a bundle, and which are incompatible.
  • Lifecycle States: Draft, Active, Retired, Obsolete. Governs the specification's operational availability.

The Many-to-One Relationship

A single Product Specification can be referenced by multiple Product Offerings, each with different pricing, branding, and channel strategies. This is the core reason for the separation:

This many-to-one pattern allows operators to innovate on commercial strategy (new offers, pricing, bundles) without redefining the underlying product each time. It also simplifies the OSS domain — the Service and Resource Catalogs only need to understand the Specification, not every variant of the Offering.

Bundles

Bundles are Product Offerings that contain other Product Offerings. A bundle offering references its component offerings, each of which references its own specification. Bundles can be:

  • Mandatory: Components that are always included and cannot be removed.
  • Optional: Components the customer can choose to add or remove.
  • Exclusive: Components that are mutually exclusive (choose one of N).

When a bundle is ordered, the COM system decomposes the bundle order into individual component orders, each of which follows its own fulfilment path through to service and resource activation.

Key insight: Bundling is a commercial construct. The Product Specification does not change when a product is bundled — only the Offering changes. This is why the PO/PS separation is essential for bundle management.

Key Takeaways

Key Takeaways

  • Product Offering is what you sell — it carries price, channel, eligibility, and bundling rules.
  • Product Specification is what the product is — it carries technical characteristics, CFS linkage, and composition rules.
  • One Specification can serve many Offerings, enabling commercial agility without technical redefinition.
  • Bundles are Offerings that contain other Offerings — they decompose at the commercial layer before touching the service layer.
  • The PO/PS separation is the foundation of catalog-driven architecture and the key to decoupling commercial innovation from network operations.
