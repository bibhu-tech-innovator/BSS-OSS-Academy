11.5 Fixed Access & Broadband

BNG/BRAS, PON access, CPE management, and IP address management

Fixed broadband access is one of the most common domains that OSS systems must orchestrate. It involves a range of network elements — from the customer premises equipment (CPE) to the aggregation and core network — each with its own management protocols and controller interfaces. This section covers the key components of fixed access orchestration.

BNG/BRAS — Broadband Network Gateway

The BNG (Broadband Network Gateway), historically called BRAS (Broadband Remote Access Server), is the aggregation point where subscriber sessions terminate. The BNG is responsible for:

  • Session management: PPPoE or IPoE session termination, authentication via RADIUS, IP address assignment via DHCP.
  • QoS enforcement: Applying traffic shaping, policing, and prioritisation per subscriber based on their service plan.
  • Accounting: Generating usage records (CDRs) for data consumption, which feed into the billing system.
  • Policy enforcement: Applying subscriber policies such as bandwidth limits, service restrictions, and lawful intercept config.

From an orchestration perspective, the BNG is typically managed via RADIUS (for subscriber authentication and policy) and NETCONF/YANG or CLI (for device configuration). Service orders for broadband often require the ROM to send RADIUS CoA (Change of Authorization) messages to update subscriber policies dynamically.

PON Access — Passive Optical Networks

PON (Passive Optical Network) is the dominant fibre access technology. The key components are:

  • OLT (Optical Line Terminal) — the head-end device in the central office that terminates the fibre and connects to the aggregation network.
  • ONT/ONU (Optical Network Terminal/Unit) — the device at the customer premises that converts optical signals to electrical.
  • Splitter — the passive optical splitter that enables a single fibre from the OLT to serve multiple premises.

PON orchestration involves provisioning the OLT to create service VLANs, binding ONTs to OLT ports, and configuring bandwidth profiles. The OLT is typically managed via NETCONF/YANG, SNMP, or a vendor-specific EMS/NMS. Service activation for a new broadband customer requires the ROM to issue commands to the OLT controller to configure the appropriate PON service profile.

CPE Management — TR-069 & USP

Customer Premises Equipment (routers, gateways, set-top boxes) must be managed remotely to reduce truck rolls and enable self-install. The dominant management protocols are:

  • TR-069 (CWMP — CPE WAN Management Protocol): The Broadband Forum standard for auto-configuration and management of CPE. The ACS (Auto-Configuration Server) communicates with the CPE over HTTPS to push configuration, firmware updates, and diagnostics.
  • USP (User Services Platform, TR-369): The next-generation CPE management protocol from the Broadband Forum. USP supports a more modern, secure, and extensible architecture than TR-069, with data model-driven configuration via YANG.

CPE management is a critical OSS function because it is often the last mile of service activation. A broadband service is not truly active until the CPE is configured with the correct SSID, VLAN, and VoIP settings. The SOM/ROM system may trigger the ACS to push the configuration after the network-side provisioning is complete.

IPAM — IP Address Management

IPAM (IP Address Management) is the system that manages IP address allocation across the operator's network. It tracks:

  • Public IP pools: Allocated to subscribers (typically /32 or /29 per subscriber).
  • Private IP ranges: Used for internal network infrastructure and CGNAT (Carrier-Grade NAT).
  • VLAN IDs: Unique per subscriber or service across the aggregation network.

IPAM integration with the orchestration layer is essential — when a service order arrives, the ROM must reserve an IP address from the IPAM system before configuring the BNG or OLT. Address exhaustion in a specific pool can block order fulfilment, making IPAM a critical dependency for broadband automation.

Key insight: Fixed broadband orchestration involves at least four distinct sub-domains — BNG, PON, CPE, and IPAM — each with its own controller, protocol, and lifecycle. The cross-domain orchestrator (XDO) must coordinate these sub-domains to deliver a complete broadband service, from the central office to the customer's router.

Key Takeaways

Key Takeaways

  • BNG/BRAS terminates subscriber sessions, enforces QoS, generates accounting data, and is managed via RADIUS and NETCONF.
  • PON access (OLT/ONT) provides fibre-to-the-premises. Orchestration involves OLT provisioning, ONT binding, and bandwidth profile configuration.
  • CPE management via TR-069 or USP enables remote configuration and firmware updates, reducing truck rolls.
  • IPAM manages IP address and VLAN pools, providing reservations that the ROM consumes during order fulfilment.
  • Broadband orchestration requires coordination across all four sub-domains, making it a classic cross-domain orchestration use case.
