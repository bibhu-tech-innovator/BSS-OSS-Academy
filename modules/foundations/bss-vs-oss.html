Section 1.1

BSS vs OSS: The Two Halves

What BSS and OSS actually are, what they own, and why the boundary between them matters.

In any telecom operator, the technology landscape splits into two fundamental domains: Business Support Systems (BSS) and Operations Support Systems (OSS). This is the first distinction a BSS/OSS architect must internalise — it governs how systems are categorised, how data flows between them, and where the handoffs between commercial and technical teams occur.

BSS — Business Support Systems

BSS systems manage the commercial relationship with the customer. They handle everything from the moment a prospect becomes a lead to the moment revenue is recognised. BSS is where products are defined, orders are captured, and bills are generated.

Customer Relationship Management (CRM)

The CRM is the front door for sales and customer service. It manages customer profiles, interactions, opportunities, cases, and provides the user interface through which agents and sometimes customers (via portals) interact with the operator's commercial systems. The CRM typically integrates with the Product Catalog to present sellable offerings and with CPQ for configuration and quoting.

Product Catalog

The Product Catalog is the authoritative source of what the operator sells. It holds Product Offerings (what the customer buys), Product Specifications (the technical definition of the product), and the structural rules that define how products bundle, discount, and depend on each other. Crucially, the Product Catalog sits in the BSS domain but references downstream OSS entities.

CPQ (Configure, Price, Quote)

CPQ is the engine that allows sales teams to configure complex product bundles — particularly in B2B scenarios — apply pricing rules, validate eligibility, and generate a commercial quote. The CPQ engine draws heavily on the Product Catalog and pricing tables to ensure every quote is accurate and deliverable.

Commercial Order Management (COM)

The COM system manages the commercial order — the customer-facing order record. It tracks the order lifecycle from capture through fulfilment and billing. The COM is responsible for order decomposition: deciding how a commercial order maps to service orders that must be executed by the OSS domain.

Billing & Rating

Billing systems consume usage records, apply rating and charging logic, generate invoices, manage accounts receivable, and handle dunning. The BSS billing function depends on the Product Catalog for pricing inputs and on the OSS for accurate service and usage data.

Product Inventory

Product Inventory holds the customer's purchased products as commercial entities. It answers questions like "Which products does customer X have?" and "What is the current state of each product?" It is distinct from Service and Resource Inventory, which sit in the OSS domain.

OSS — Operations Support Systems

OSS systems manage the technical delivery and operation of the network and services. They design, provision, monitor, and maintain the services and resources that make the operator's products work.

Service Catalog

The Service Catalog defines the services the operator can deliver. It contains Customer-Facing Services (CFS) — the services the customer is aware of — and Resource-Facing Services (RFS) — the internal technical services that compose the CFS. The Service Catalog is the bridge between what is sold and what is built.

Resource Catalog

The Resource Catalog defines the physical and logical resources available in the network: devices, ports, VLANs, IP addresses, virtual network functions, and more. It describes the capabilities and properties of each resource type and the rules for how they can be combined.

Service Order Management (SOM)

SOM receives decomposed service orders from the COM and manages their fulfilment. SOM orchestrates the service-level activities — validating service feasibility, allocating service parameters, and instructing the resource layer to configure the network.

Resource Order Management (ROM)

ROM is the OSS system that manages orders against network resources. A resource order might instruct a domain controller to create a VLAN, configure a router interface, or spin up a virtual network function. ROM is the last order management layer before the actual network.

Service Level Management (SLM)

SLM monitors service performance against contractual targets. It collects KPIs from the network, calculates SLA compliance, and feeds assurance data back to the BSS domain so that billing can apply credits or generate reports. SLM sits at the boundary between Fulfilment and Assurance.

Resource Inventory

Resource Inventory holds the actual state of all network resources — physical equipment, logical connections, IP allocations, and topology relationships. It is the OSS counterpart to Product Inventory and is the source of truth for what exists in the network.

Why the Boundary Matters

Understanding the BSS/OSS boundary is not an academic exercise. It has real architectural and operational consequences:

  • Data ownership: BSS owns the customer and product data. OSS owns the service and resource data. Crossing these boundaries without clear API contracts leads to data inconsistency.
  • System lifecycle: BSS systems change with commercial strategy (new products, pricing changes). OSS systems change with network technology (new equipment, protocols). Different cadences mean different release cycles.
  • Organizational alignment: BSS typically aligns with Marketing, Sales, and Finance. OSS aligns with Network Engineering and Operations. The boundary is as much about people and process as it is about technology.
  • Integration complexity: Every flow that crosses the BSS/OSS boundary — an order, a usage record, a service test result — must be carefully mapped to ensure semantics are preserved on both sides.
  • Vendor landscape: Some vendors cover both BSS and OSS; others specialise. Understanding the boundary helps evaluate vendor coverage accurately.

Key Takeaways

📌 Key Takeaways

  • BSS manages the commercial relationship (customer, product, order, billing); OSS manages the technical delivery (service, resource, network, assurance).
  • Key BSS systems: CRM, Product Catalog, CPQ, COM, Billing, Product Inventory.
  • Key OSS systems: Service Catalog, Resource Catalog, SOM, ROM, SLM, Resource Inventory.
  • The boundary is defined by data ownership, system lifecycle, organisational structure, and integration patterns.
  • Every order that flows from BSS to OSS must cross this boundary — making catalog-driven decomposition the critical integration pattern.
