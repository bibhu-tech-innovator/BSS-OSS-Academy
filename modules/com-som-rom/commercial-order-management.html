3.2 Commercial Order Management (COM)

Order Capture, Validation, and Decomposition

Commercial Order Management (COM) is the topmost layer of the three-layer order model. It is the system that captures what the customer wants to buy, validates commercial viability, calculates pricing, obtains approvals, and initiates the downstream fulfilment chain. COM operates in the language of the business — product offerings, prices, discounts, contracts, and customer accounts — shielding the technical layers from commercial complexity.

The Order Capture Process

Order capture is the process of collecting and validating all information needed to create a valid commercial order. It typically begins in a CRM, CPQ (Configure-Price-Quote), or customer self-care portal and proceeds through several stages before the order is submitted to the COM engine for processing.

Customer Selects Offer → Configure & Characterise → Price & Validate → Approve → Submit Order

1. Customer Selects Offer

The customer (or agent acting on their behalf) browses the product catalog and selects one or more product offerings. The COM system retrieves the product offering specification from the Product Catalog (TMF620) and presents the relevant characteristics, eligibility rules, and pricing to the customer.

2. Configure and Characterise

For each selected product offering, the customer provides the specific characteristics required to fulfil the order. For example, for a broadband offering, this might include the installation address, desired speed tier, and modem type. The COM system validates that the provided characteristics conform to the specification's allowed values and required attributes.

3. Price and Validate

The COM system calculates the one-time and recurring charges based on the product offering pricing, any promotions or discounts, and the customer's specific characteristics. Commercial validation includes:

  • Eligibility: Is the customer eligible for this offer (e.g., credit check, geographic availability, existing contract terms)?
  • Pre-qualification: Can the requested service be delivered at the specified location (e.g., address validation, port availability)?
  • Bundle consistency: If the order includes multiple products, are there any conflicts or dependency violations?
  • Credit limit: Does the order value exceed the customer's approved credit limit or deposit threshold?

4. Approve

Depending on the order value, customer segment, and business rules, the order may require manual or automated approval. Approvals may involve credit management, legal review, or technical feasibility checks. In many modern COM systems, low-risk orders are auto-approved, while high-value or complex orders are routed to a workflow for manual review.

5. Submit Order

Once approved, the order is submitted to the COM orchestration engine as a TMF622 Product Order. The COM engine validates the order structure, records it in the order database, initiates the commercial order lifecycle, and begins the decomposition process that will trigger downstream service and resource orders.

Note: In a pure catalog-driven architecture, the COM engine does not need to know anything about how services are designed or how the network is configured. It only needs to know the product offering-to-service specification mapping rules defined in the catalog. This is the fundamental decoupling that enables commercial agility.

Commercial Order Types in Detail

The COM layer handles several distinct order types, each following a slightly different workflow. The type is typically determined by the orderType attribute in the TMF622 payload.

Order Type TMF622 Action Description Decomposition Behaviour
Install add Creating a new product instance and associated services. This is the most common order type. Creates new service order items for each CFS. Decomposition runs the full lifecycle from product to network activation.
Change modify Changing characteristics of an existing product (e.g., speed upgrade, feature enablement). Creates service order items with modify action on affected CFS. Only changed characteristics are propagated.
Disconnect delete Terminating a product instance and decommissioning associated services. Creates service order items with delete action. SOM coordinates graceful teardown, and ROM releases resources.
Transfer transfer Transferring ownership of a product from one customer to another. Creates service order items with modify for ownership attributes. May or may not involve resource changes.
Suspend suspend Temporarily disabling a service while preserving the product and service configuration. Creates service order items with suspend action. ROM may apply administrative down or traffic filtering.
Restore restore Re-enabling a previously suspended service, returning it to active operation. Creates service order items with restore action. ROM reverses any suspension configurations.

Commercial Order Lifecycle States

The TMF622 Product Ordering API defines a standard state machine for commercial orders. While implementations may extend this model with additional intermediate states, the core states and transitions are consistent across all TM Forum-compliant COM systems.

State Description Valid Transitions To Business Significance
Submitted The order has been received and accepted by the COM engine. Initial validation is complete. In Progress, Rejected The order enters the system. No downstream activity has started.
Rejected The order failed initial validation (e.g., missing mandatory characteristics, invalid eligibility). Submitted (if resubmitted) Customer or agent must correct the order and resubmit.
In Progress The order is being processed. Decomposition has started, and service orders are being created. Pending, Held, Completed, Cancelled, Failed Primary processing state. Downstream SOM/ROM orders are generated.
Pending The order is waiting for an external event or manual intervention (e.g., credit approval, third-party delivery). In Progress, Held, Cancelled Order is blocked by an external dependency. Timer-driven escalation may apply.
Held The order has been deliberately paused, typically at the customer's request or due to a business hold. In Progress, Cancelled No work is being done. Any allocated resources may be temporarily reserved or released.
Completed All order items have been successfully fulfilled. Products are active and billing has started. Terminal state Success. The order is closed and all downstream processes have completed.
Failed One or more order items could not be fulfilled. Compensation / rollback has been executed. Terminal state Failure. Customer must be notified, and a new order may be needed.
Cancelled The order was terminated before completion, either by customer request or automated timeout. Terminal state Cancellation. Any work-in-progress in downstream layers must be compensated.

TMF622 Product Ordering API in Depth

The TMF622 Product Ordering API is the standard interface for creating, reading, updating, and deleting commercial orders. It is the API that CRM, CPQ, and self-care portals use to submit orders to the COM engine. Understanding its structure is essential for designing COM interfaces.

Key TMF622 Resources

  • ProductOrder: The top-level order resource containing order header information (customer reference, order date, billing account, order type) and a list of order items.
  • ProductOrderItem: A line item within the order, referencing a product offering, specifying the action (add, modify, delete, etc.), and providing the product characteristics and pricing.
  • Product: The product being ordered. For an add action, the product characteristics are specified. For modify and delete actions, the existing product ID is referenced.
  • RelatedParty: References to the customer, bill payer, and other parties associated with the order.
  • OrderItemRelationship: Defines dependencies between order items (e.g., item B requires item A to be completed first).
  • BillingAccountRef: Reference to the billing account that will be charged for the product.
  • PaymentRef: Payment method and terms for the order.

Standard TMF622 REST Endpoints

  • POST /productOrder — Create a new product order.
  • GET /productOrder/{id} — Retrieve an existing product order by ID.
  • GET /productOrder — List product orders with optional filtering (by status, date range, customer ID).
  • PATCH /productOrder/{id} — Update an existing order (e.g., change characteristics, cancel an item).
  • DELETE /productOrder/{id} — Delete or cancel an order (typically only allowed in certain states).

Notification Events (CloudEvents / Callbacks)

TMF622 defines a set of notification events that the COM engine emits when order state changes occur. Downstream systems (and UI dashboards) subscribe to these events to stay synchronised:

  • productOrderCreationNotification — A new order has been created.
  • productOrderStateChangeNotification — The order state has transitioned (e.g., from In Progress to Completed).
  • productOrderItemStateChangeNotification — A specific order item's state has changed.
  • productOrderInformationRequiredNotification — The order is blocked waiting for additional information.

Decomposition Mapping: From Product Offering to Service

The critical function of the COM layer is decomposition: translating each product offering order item into one or more service order items that can be fulfilled by the SOM layer. This mapping is defined in the catalog through the relationship between Product Specifications and Service Specifications.

How Decomposition Works

When a COM order item enters the In Progress state, the COM engine performs the following steps:

  1. Retrieve the Product Specification from the Product Catalog (TMF620) for the ordered product offering.
  2. Identify the Service Specification relationships: The product specification defines which CFS (Customer Facing Service) specifications are required to fulfil it. For example, a "Business MPLS Bundle" product might reference three CFS specifications: "MPLS Port CFS", "MPLS VPN CFS", and "Managed Router CFS".
  3. Map characteristics: The COM engine maps the product characteristics from the order item to the corresponding service characteristics. For example, a product-level "Speed Tier" characteristic (e.g., "100Mbps") maps to a CFS-level "Bandwidth" characteristic.
  4. Create service order items: For each CFS specification identified, the COM engine creates a service order item with the appropriate action (add/modify/delete) and the mapped characteristics.
  5. Submit the service order: The COM engine submits a TMF641 Service Order to the SOM layer, containing all of the service order items and their interdependencies.

Characteristic Mapping Table Example

The following table illustrates how characteristics from a product offering are mapped to characteristics on the underlying CFS specifications during decomposition:

Product Characteristic Product Offering (Example) Target CFS Service Characteristic Mapping Rule
Speed Tier Business Broadband 100Mbps Broadband CFS Download Speed Direct mapping (value = "100Mbps")
Speed Tier Business Broadband 100Mbps Broadband CFS Upload Speed Derived (upload = download * ratio per tier)
Installation Address Business Broadband 100Mbps Broadband CFS Service Address Direct mapping (full address structure)
Modem Type Business Broadband 100Mbps CFS CPE Management CPE Model Direct mapping (from allowed values)
Static IP Count Business Broadband 100Mbps Broadband CFS IP Address Count Direct mapping (integer value)
Contract Term Business Broadband 100Mbps Not mapped to service — Consumed by COM for pricing; not propagated to service layer
Promotion Code Business Broadband 100Mbps Not mapped to service — Consumed by COM for discount calculation; not propagated
Billing Cycle Business Broadband 100Mbps Not mapped to service — Consumed by Billing; not relevant to service fulfilment

As the table demonstrates, not all product characteristics are propagated to the service layer. COM characteristics that are purely commercial (pricing, contract terms, billing preferences) are consumed by the COM and Billing systems and are not relevant to service fulfilment. Only characteristics that affect service design and network configuration are passed down to SOM.

Error Handling and Compensation in COM

When a commercial order item fails (either during decomposition or because a downstream service order fails), the COM engine must execute compensation logic to undo any partial work. The compensation strategy depends on the nature of the failure and the order state:

  • Pre-decomposition failure: If the order fails before any service orders are created (e.g., validation failure), the order transitions directly to Failed without any downstream impact.
  • Post-decomposition failure: If one or more service orders are already in progress when a failure occurs, the COM engine must:
    • Send cancellation requests to the SOM layer for any in-progress service orders.
    • Reverse any billing adjustments that may have been applied (e.g., prorated charges).
    • Update the product order item state to reflect the failure.
  • Partial failure: In multi-item orders, some items may succeed while others fail. The COM engine may mark failed items as Failed while keeping successful items Completed, or it may fail the entire order depending on business rules and item dependencies.

Best practice: COM systems should implement the Saga pattern for distributed transaction management. Each step in the order process has a corresponding compensating action that can undo it. The saga coordinator tracks the state of each step and, if a failure occurs, executes the compensating actions in reverse order.

COM Integration Patterns

COM systems rarely exist in isolation. They integrate with multiple upstream and downstream systems in the BSS/OSS landscape:

  • CRM (Upstream): The CRM system is typically the system of engagement for order capture. It submits TMF622 orders to COM via the Product Ordering API. COM publishes order state changes back to CRM for customer-facing dashboards.
  • Product Catalog (Reference): COM reads product offering and specification data from the Product Catalog (TMF620) to drive the order capture experience and decomposition logic.
  • CPQ (Upstream): In enterprise B2B scenarios, CPQ systems handle complex configuration and pricing before submitting the approved order to COM.
  • SOM (Downstream): COM submits TMF641 Service Orders to SOM for fulfilment and receives state change notifications back from SOM.
  • Billing (Downstream / Parallel): COM may send product activation events to the billing system to trigger bill start dates and one-time charges.
  • Document Management (Reference): COM may store or reference signed contracts, SOWs, and supporting documents via TMF667.
  • Party / Customer Management (Reference): COM validates customer data via TMF629 and creates or updates party records as needed.

Key Takeaways

  • COM is the business-facing layer that captures, validates, and manages commercial orders in the language of product offerings, pricing, and customer accounts.
  • Order capture follows a standard five-stage process: offer selection, configuration, pricing/validation, approval, and submission.
  • TMF622 Product Ordering API provides the standardised interface for commercial order management, defining resources, endpoints, state machines, and notification events.
  • Decomposition is the core value-adding function of COM — translating product order items into service order items by following catalog-defined mappings.
  • Not all product characteristics are propagated to the service layer; purely commercial attributes are consumed by COM and billing systems.
  • Error handling and compensation must be explicitly designed, ideally following the Saga pattern, to gracefully handle failures at any point in the order lifecycle.
  • COM integrates with CRM/CPQ upstream and SOM, Billing, and other systems downstream via standard TM Forum APIs.
  • The product catalog is the authoritative source of decomposition rules, enabling commercial teams to create new offers without involving the service or network layers.
