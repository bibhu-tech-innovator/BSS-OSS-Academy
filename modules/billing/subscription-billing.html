Subscription Billing & SLM

Recurring charges, SLM dependency, proration, and the bill run process.

Recurring Charges

Subscription billing handles all charges that recur on a predictable schedule — typically monthly, but also quarterly, annually, or custom billing periods. Recurring charges include:

  • Line rental / access fees — The base fee for having a service connection active.
  • Plan charges — The monthly cost of a mobile, broadband, or TV plan.
  • Feature charges — Add-on services such as international calling packs, data top-ups, or premium content subscriptions.
  • Equipment instalments — Handset or CPE payments spread across the contract term.
  • Discounts and credits — Loyalty discounts, bundle discounts, promotional credits applied to the recurring charge.

SLM Dependency for SLA-Based Billing

Service Level Management (SLM) is the system that tracks whether a service is meeting its contracted service level agreement (SLA). In SLA-based billing, the recurring charge may be adjusted based on SLM-measured performance:

  • Availability credits — If service uptime falls below the SLA threshold (e.g., 99.9%), the customer receives a credit calculated as a percentage of the monthly recurring charge.
  • Performance credits — If latency, jitter, or throughput falls below guaranteed levels, credits are applied based on the severity and duration of the breach.
  • Billing adjustments — SLM provides a credit recommendation to the billing system, which generates a credit note or line-item adjustment on the next invoice.
  • Escalation-based refunds — For severe or repeated SLA breaches, SLM may trigger escalated credits or contract penalties that flow through to billing.

The SLM-to-billing integration is typically mediated by a service credit management system or directly via TMF APIs (TMF 678 Customer Bill Management). The critical requirement is that credit calculations are auditable: every credit must be traceable to the specific SLA violation event.

Proration

When a service is activated, suspended, or terminated mid-cycle, charges must be prorated to reflect the partial period:

  • Activation proration — Service activated on the 15th of the month. The recurring charge is prorated from the 15th to the end of the billing period. The formula is: full charge * (days remaining / total days in period).
  • Cancellation proration — Service terminated on the 10th. The recurring charge for the final period is prorated up to the termination date. Early termination fees may also apply.
  • Suspension proration — Service suspended due to non-payment. The recurring charge may be stopped or reduced during the suspension period, depending on policy.
  • Plan change proration — Customer upgrades from Plan A to Plan B mid-cycle. The old plan is charged up to the change date, the new plan is charged from the change date. A credit is applied for the unused portion of the old plan.

The Bill Run Process

The bill run is the scheduled process that generates customer invoices for a billing period. It is one of the most operationally critical processes in any telco:

  1. Cycle start — The billing system determines which customers are due for invoicing based on their billing cycle (e.g., all customers with cycle day 1 are billed on the 1st of the month).
  2. Recurring charge calculation — All recurring charges for the period are calculated, including proration for mid-cycle changes.
  3. Usage charge aggregation — Rated usage events from the mediation pipeline are aggregated by subscriber and service.
  4. Discount and adjustment application — Promotional discounts, loyalty credits, and SLA-based service credits are applied.
  5. Tax calculation — Applicable taxes are computed based on the customer's jurisdiction, service type, and regulatory requirements.
  6. Invoice generation — The invoice is generated in the required format (PDF, e-Bill, structured data for ERP).
  7. Invoice delivery — Invoices are distributed via the customer's preferred channel (email, portal, postal mail).
  8. Post-run processing — Journal entries are posted to the general ledger, accounts receivable is updated, and notifications are sent.

A single bill run may generate millions of invoices. Errors are costly — both financially (incorrect charges lead to credits and regulatory penalties) and reputationally (customers notice billing errors immediately). This is why billing system changes are treated with extreme caution.

Key Takeaways

  • Subscription billing handles recurring charges for line rental, plans, features, equipment, and discounts.
  • SLM integration enables SLA-based billing with automated credit calculation for service level breaches.
  • Proration ensures fair charging for mid-cycle activations, cancellations, suspensions, and plan changes.
  • The bill run is the most operationally critical billing process, generating millions of invoices per cycle.
