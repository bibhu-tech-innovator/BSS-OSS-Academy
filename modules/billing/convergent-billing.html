5.6 Convergent Billing

Prepaid/postpaid convergence

Traditionally, telecom operators maintained separate billing systems for prepaid (pay-in-advance) and postpaid (pay-after-use) customers. Prepaid required real-time rating and balance management via an Online Charging System (OCS), while postpaid relied on batch rating and invoice generation. Convergent billing — also called unified billing — replaces this dual-stack architecture with a single platform that handles both prepaid and postpaid, across fixed, mobile, and broadband services.

The Dual-Stack Problem

Most operators with legacy architectures run separate stacks for prepaid and postpaid:

  • Prepaid stack: OCS + real-time rating + balance management. Every usage event is rated and checked against the subscriber's balance in real-time before the service is delivered.
  • Postpaid stack: Mediation + batch rating + invoice generation. Usage is collected, mediated, rated in batch, and billed to the subscriber at the end of the billing cycle.

The dual-stack approach creates several problems: higher operational costs from managing two platforms, inability to offer hybrid plans (e.g., postpaid with prepaid data top-ups), inconsistent customer experience across payment methods, and complex integration for converged services like quad-play bundles.

Single Balance Architecture

The core concept of convergent billing is the single balance architecture. Instead of maintaining separate balances in the OCS (for prepaid) and the billing system (for postpaid), convergent billing uses a single logical balance that can be:

  • Topped up in real-time (prepaid behaviour).
  • Invoiced at end of cycle (postpaid behaviour).
  • Shared across services (voice, SMS, data, VAS).
  • Monitored for threshold alerts and spending limits.

This single balance is managed by the OCS, which has evolved from a pure prepaid platform into a convergent charging engine that handles both real-time and deferred charging scenarios.

Unified Rating

Convergent billing requires a unified rating engine that can apply the same rating logic regardless of whether the subscriber is prepaid or postpaid. The rating engine must support:

  • Real-time rating: For prepaid, the rating must complete within milliseconds so the OCS can grant or deny service.
  • Batch rating: For postpaid, the same rating logic is applied in batch after usage is collected.
  • Hybrid rating: For subscribers with mixed plans (e.g., postpaid base with prepaid top-ups), the rating engine must determine which balance to draw from based on the plan configuration.

Unified rating eliminates the need to maintain two sets of rating tables and ensures that pricing is consistent across all customer segments.

OCS Role in Convergent Charging

The OCS is the central component of convergent billing. It handles:

  • Session-based charging: For voice calls and data sessions, the OCS interacts with network elements (via Diameter Gy/Ro interfaces) to authorise, monitor, and terminate sessions based on balance.
  • Event-based charging: For SMS, MMS, and single events, the OCS performs a one-time rating and balance deduction.
  • Reservation: The OCS reserves funds from the balance for in-progress sessions and releases unspent reservations on session termination.
  • Notifications: The OCS triggers real-time SMS or push notifications when balances reach thresholds.

Key insight: Modern convergent billing systems have blurred the line between OCS and traditional billing. The OCS now handles not just prepaid balance management, but also credit limits, spending controls, and real-time notifications for postpaid subscribers. The ultimate goal is a single charging and billing platform for all subscriber types and services.

Key Takeaways

Key Takeaways

  • The dual-stack problem — separate prepaid and postpaid systems — leads to high costs, inflexibility, and inconsistent customer experience.
  • Single balance architecture unifies prepaid and postpaid balances in one logical entity managed by the OCS.
  • Unified rating applies the same rating logic across all payment methods, supporting real-time, batch, and hybrid scenarios.
  • The OCS evolves from a prepaid platform into a convergent charging engine that handles session, event, and reservation-based charging for all subscribers.
  • Convergent billing enables hybrid plans, multi-service bundles, and consistent charging across fixed, mobile, and broadband services.
