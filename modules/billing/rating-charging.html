Rating & Charging

How usage events are valued and how value is deducted from subscriber balances.

Rating vs Charging

Rating and charging are two distinct but closely related functions:

  • Rating is the process of determining the monetary value of a usage event based on pricing rules. Given a call duration of 5 minutes, plan "Premium Unlimited", and a destination of "mobile national", the rating engine calculates a price of $0.00 (included) or $0.05 (overage).
  • Charging is the process of applying the rated value to the subscriber's financial balance or generating a debt. For pre-paid subscribers, charging deducts from a prepaid balance. For post-paid, it creates a charge item on the next invoice.

Real-Time vs Batch Rating

The timing of rating and charging splits the billing world into two fundamental modes:

CharacteristicReal-Time (Online)Batch (Offline)
When rating occursDuring the service session (before or during service delivery)After the service session (CDR-based, batch processing)
Credit controlYes — balance is checked and reserved before service is grantedNo — service is delivered first, CDR is rated later
ProtocolsDiameter (Ro, Rf), CAP, IN, HTTP (API-based)FTP/SFTP-based CDR file transfer, Kafka streaming
Typical servicesPre-paid mobile voice, data, top-ups, IMS servicesPost-paid fixed broadband, enterprise VPN, contract mobile
SystemOnline Charging System (OCS)Offline Charging System (OFCS) / Billing System
Latency requirementSub-second for credit checkMinutes to hours for batch processing

Event Types

Billing events come in several forms, each requiring different rating logic:

  • Session-based events — Voice calls, data sessions, video streams. These have duration, volume, start/end time, and QoS attributes. Rating is based on time (per-minute) or volume (per-MB/GB), often with a rounding granularity (e.g., per-second after first minute).
  • Transaction-based events — SMS, MMS, content purchases, value-added service requests. Each transaction is a discrete chargeable event with a fixed or tiered price.
  • Recurring events — Monthly subscription fees, line rental, feature charges. These are not usage-based but are rated based on the product catalog price and proration rules.
  • One-time events — Activation fees, installation charges, termination fees. A fixed charge applied once per order or once per lifecycle event.

Online Charging System (OCS) Architecture

The OCS is the central platform for real-time credit control. Its key components are:

  • Session Manager — Manages Diameter-based credit control sessions (RFC 4006) with network elements. Tracks session state, balance consumption, and re-authorisation triggers.
  • Rating Engine — Applies pricing rules to determine the unit cost of the current session. Handles time-of-day discounts, volume tiers, bundle exhaustion, and cross-product discount sharing.
  • Account Balance Manager — Maintains real-time subscriber balances (main balance, top-up balances, bonus accounts, loyalty points). Enforces balance expiry, stacking rules, and usage priority.
  • Reservation Manager — Reserves a portion of the subscriber's balance when a session starts (initial reservation), and requests top-ups as the reservation is consumed. Releases unused reservation on session end.
  • Charging Gateway — Generates charging data records (CDRs) from the session for offline processing, billing, and revenue assurance audit trails.

Convergent OCS solutions unify pre-paid and post-paid charging on a single platform. A post-paid subscriber may still interact with the OCS for usage tracking (rating is performed, but charging creates a debt rather than deducting from a prepaid balance). This convergent model enables hybrid plans — post-paid with spending caps and real-time usage notifications.

Key Takeaways

  • Rating determines the value of usage; charging applies that value to a subscriber's balance or invoice.
  • Real-time rating (via OCS) is essential for pre-paid services; batch rating handles post-paid and enterprise billing.
  • OCS architecture comprises session management, rating, balance management, reservation, and charging gateway components.
  • Convergent charging unifies pre-paid and post-paid on a single platform, enabling hybrid billing models.
